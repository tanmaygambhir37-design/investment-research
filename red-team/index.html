Adversarial Review

Red-Team Review: Both Models

I built these models to learn, but a model is only useful if it survives being attacked. I ran an adversarial review on my own work, pretending I was a skeptical Investment Committee member trying to break the thesis before approving the deal. Where a challenge landed, I changed the numbers. Where it didn't, the defense is on the record.

Why this document exists

Investment committees don't take a model on faith. They pay someone to attack it. I did exactly that to my own work. Some attacks landed and I changed the numbers: the Discounted Cash Flow (DCF) rating fell from BUY to HOLD, and the Leveraged Buyout (LBO) headline story got rewritten. Some attacks didn't land, and the defense is on the record. This page is the paper trail of changing my mind for the right reasons.

If you only read two things:

  1. Recordati, challenge B1: The acquisition strategy (buying smaller drug companies) everyone credits for pharma buyouts adds roughly zero to the return rate. The whole investment case is a bet that the exit price holds.
  2. Technogym, challenge A1: My own model was hiding the software business inside a blended margin. Separating it out showed the software is worth ~€3.30 a share, five times what I first claimed. The model beat my own critique.

Part A: Technogym DCF (base €15.72, HOLD)

A previous accounting fix (charging lease principal repayments in the free cash flow build) had already moved the base fair value to €15.75. This review additionally isolates the digital Profit & Loss (P&L). Hardware profit margin expands on its own operating leverage to ~20.75%, while digital revenue carries its own ~60% margin. Net effect: base fair value is €15.72 / HOLD (+3.5%), with a bear case of €8.82 and a bull case of €24.05. Isolating digital reveals the option is worth ~€3.3/share (not the ~€0.6 it showed when blended). This is a redistribution of existing value, so the total base margin is held at the defensible ~23.5% and the rating does not change.

Challenges that land (and what they change)

A1. "Your margin endpoint is doing the work of a thesis." 23.5% by 2032 assumes seven more years of net expansion after four exceptional ones. Procurement renegotiation and re-engineering are one-time gains that annualise out; what's left is mix, and the mix shift depends on the digital/services growth you yourself call an option. If margins plateau at 22.5%, fair value drops ~€1.3. Verdict: lands, and this revision acts on it. Digital is now isolated at its own margin, so the total-margin endpoint no longer smuggles the mix shift into hardware. Base total margin holds at ~23.5% (hardware ~20.75% standalone + digital ~60%); the honest central read is €15.5–16.0, not a €17.34 point. Present it as HOLD.

A2. "You answered the drawdown with a hypothesis, not evidence." The tariff-deferral explanation for Americas +2.5% is unverified. If it's share loss (Matrix/Life Fitness price aggression), the BtoB fade path is too generous. Verdict: lands as a timing issue. H1 results print 30 July. Recommendation is conditioned: a BUY is only actionable after the H1 print confirms Americas stabilisation. This is now in the investment summary.

A3. "A 63% terminal-value share means your explicit period barely matters, and your 2.25% terminal growth embeds an immortality assumption for a consumer-capex brand." Verdict: does not change the number but changes the presentation: the exit-multiple cross-check (Gordon-implied ~9.4x, below today's ~13x) is the stronger defence and is now cited first.

A4. "Beta 0.95 triangulated is soft." True: providers range 0.80–1.16, and the WACC sensitivity band (7.0–8.75%) swings value €14.3–22.6. Verdict: acceptable because the grid makes the dependence transparent; noted as a known limitation.

Challenges that don't land

  • "Digital estimate is invented." It is an explicitly labelled low-confidence estimation block with editable drivers, built precisely because the company won't disclose. The alternative, pretending the platform question can't be analysed, is worse.
  • "Comps are junk." Correct that no clean comp exists; the model uses them only for triangulation and says so. Johnson Health Tech at 10.5x and Garmin/Amer at ~20x bracket the debate accurately.
  • "Net cash should fund a buyback assumption." Speculative capital allocation is not underwritten in a DCF; the authorisation is noted as optionality.

Revised judgment: Technogym

Rating: HOLD. Fair value €15.72 (+3.5% vs €15.19): below the >20% BUY hurdle. A BUY would require an H1-26 print (30 July 2026) confirming Americas stabilisation and a credible first digital-KPI disclosure; entry attractive below €14.50 with margin of safety; conviction MODERATE (6.5/10). The digital option (~€3.3/share) is real but sits in the bull case, not the base. What would change my mind to BUY: Americas re-acceleration plus first digital disclosure. To bear: Americas share loss; first margin decline in 5 years.

Part B: Recordati LBO (base ~15% IRR / ~2.0x, PARTICIPATE selectively)

Reconciliation (18 July 2026): two mechanical fixes. (i) The 2031 exit-year BD spend (€400m that bought EBITDA only in 2032, post-exit) is removed, cutting cumulative BD to €1.6bn at a true 9.0x effective. (ii) Net debt refinanced is bridged from the 2025 reported gross €2,037m to a close-date estimate ~€1,750m (2026 free cash flow less dividends). Net: base ~15% IRR / ~2.0x MOIC, entry 12.6x on close-date net debt (12.9x announced basis). BD value-creation decomposition: no-BD floor ~15%; funding BD €400m/yr adds ~0 to −0.1pt (the 9x-in/11.5x-out arbitrage is offset by interest drag on un-swept cash plus forfeited deleveraging); exit at entry-flat 12.9x adds +2.9pt. The exit multiple is ~100% of the sponsor-consistent case: BD grows MOIC dollars, not the rate.

Challenges that land

B1. "Your base case both haircuts the exit multiple AND removes the BD machine: that's double conservatism, and it makes the model unable to explain why sophisticated buyers did the deal." Verdict: lands, but this review corrects the diagnosis. The two levers are NOT worth the same: the decomposition above shows BD ≈ 0pt and the exit multiple ≈ +2.9pt, so they are not substitutes ("entry-flat exit OR funded BD" was wrong; the "OR" is deleted). The investment summary now presents: Conservative no-BD floor (~15%), Sponsor-consistent (entry-flat 12.9x exit: ~18%), Full-thesis (12.9x + 6.0x leverage + bull ops: ~24%). The recommendation stands; the framing is corrected: the sponsor case is a bet on exit-multiple maintenance, not on the bolt-on machine.

B2. "Your change-in-working-capital assumption at 15% of revenue growth is a guess sitting on the fastest-moving part of the model (US rare-disease inventory absorbed €135m in 2025 alone)." Verdict: lands as a sensitivity gap. At 25% of revenue change, cumulative free cash flow drops ~€200m and exit leverage rises ~0.15x, an IRR impact of ~−0.6pt. Material but not thesis-breaking; flagged as a build-time refinement (the input is editable in the workbook).

B3. "Single-flow IRR flatters nothing but hides refinancing/dividend-recap optionality, and conversely ignores the 2026 stub period." We close 31 December 2026 using 2026 guidance as the entry year; if closing slips into 2027 or 2026 misses guidance, entry economics shift. Verdict: partially lands; documented as a structural simplification. Recap optionality would only improve IRR (~+1pt), a conservative direction.

B4. "Isturisa is ~35–40% of Rare Diseases by our own estimate, and relacorilant is a live Phase 3 threat. Where's the product-level bear?" Verdict: lands directionally. The bear scenario (Rare Diseases growth +8%→5%) is the proxy; a true product-level model needs disclosure Recordati doesn't provide. Recorded as a known limitation with the register's low-confidence label discipline.

Challenges that don't land

  • "12.9x is simply too expensive: Ipsen is 8.4x." The market cleared this exact multiple twice (2018, 2026), with the most sophisticated possible seller-buyer pair. Ipsen is the challenge case, not the proof; the sensitivity grid prices it (at 9–10x exit, IRR 2–7%: that IS the risk statement).
  • "100% cash sweep is unrealistic." It's the conservative modeling default; covenant-lite reality would allow retention, which only reallocates value, and the sweep input is editable.
  • "You can't validate against the actual debt structure." Correct until the CONSOB offer document publishes; the model's €5.45bn at the bottom of the reported €5.5–6bn range is the right independent posture.

Revised judgment: Recordati

Rating: PARTICIPATE as co-invest (6/10), unchanged in direction but reframed: our ~15% is a conservative floor, not a midpoint; the sponsor-consistent read is ~18% and comes entirely from holding the exit multiple at entry (12.9x), not from BD. Decline as a standalone sponsor underwrite at a 20%+ hurdle without conviction that the exit multiple holds at entry: the decomposition shows that is the bet, not the bolt-on machine. What would change my mind positively: relacorilant failure, offer document showing cheaper debt, first post-close bolt-on at <9x. Negatively: 2026 margin miss below 36%, Isturisa US script deceleration.

Part C: Structural weaknesses common to both (recorded, not fixable in-scope)

  1. No mid-year discounting convention (DCF): year-end discounting understates value ~3–4%; direction is conservative, documented.
  2. Provider-triangulated beta, not regression-computed (licensed price series unavailable in build environment).
  3. Grid approximations on both sensitivity sheets, documented in-sheet with bias direction.
  4. No annotated source screenshots: table-level citations substitute; primary PDFs linked in the bibliography.
  5. Aggregator dependence for Recordati 2021–24 historicals (standardised vs company-adjusted EBITDA reconciled by note, not by rebuild from annual reports).
  6. H1-26 results (Technogym, 30 July) and the CONSOB offer document (Recordati, ~Q3-26) both land within weeks: both models carry explicit rebase triggers.

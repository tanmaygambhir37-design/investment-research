Challenges that land (and what they change)

A1. "Your margin endpoint is doing the work of a thesis." 23.5% by 2032 assumes seven more years of net expansion after four exceptional ones. Procurement renegotiation and re-engineering are one-time gains that annualise out; what's left is mix — and the mix shift depends on the digital/services growth you yourself call an option. If margins plateau at 22.5%, fair value drops ~€1.3. Verdict: lands, and v3 acts on it. Digital is now isolated at its own margin, so the total-margin endpoint no longer smuggles the mix shift into hardware. Base total margin holds at ~23.5% (hardware ~20.75% standalone + digital ~60%); the honest central read is €15.5–16.0, not a €17.34 point. Present it as HOLD.

A2. "You answered the drawdown with a hypothesis, not evidence." The tariff-deferral explanation for Americas +2.5% is unverified. If it's share loss (Matrix/Life Fitness price aggression), the BtoB fade path is too generous. Verdict: lands as a timing issue. H1 results print 30 July. Recommendation is conditioned: a BUY is only actionable after the H1 print confirms Americas stabilisation. This is now in the IC summary.

A3. "A 63% TV share means your explicit period barely matters — and your g of 2.25% embeds an immortality assumption for a consumer-capex brand." Verdict: does not change the number but changes the presentation: the exit-multiple cross-check (Gordon-implied ~9.4x — below today's ~13x) is the stronger defence and is now cited first.

A4. "Beta 0.95 triangulated is soft." True: providers range 0.80–1.16, and the WACC sensitivity band (7.0–8.75%) swings value €14.3–22.6. Verdict: acceptable because the grid makes the dependence transparent; noted as a known limitation.

Challenges that don't land

  • "Digital estimate is invented." It is an explicitly labelled L-confidence estimation block with editable drivers, built precisely because the company won't disclose. The alternative — pretending the platform question can't be analysed — is worse.
  • "Comps are junk." Correct that no clean comp exists; the model uses them only for triangulation and says so. JHT 10.5x and Garmin/Amer ~20x bracket the debate accurately.
  • "Net cash should fund a buyback assumption." Speculative capital allocation is not underwritten in a DCF; the authorisation is noted as optionality.

v3 reconciliation (18-Jul-26): two mechanical fixes — (i) the 2031 exit-year BD spend (€400m that bought EBITDA only in 2032, post-exit) is removed, cutting cumulative BD to €1.6bn at a true 9.0x effective; (ii) net debt refinanced is bridged from FY25A gross €2,037m to a close-date estimate ~€1,750m (2026 FCF less dividends). Challenges that land

B1. "Your base case both haircuts the exit multiple AND removes the BD machine — that's double conservatism, and it makes the model unable to explain why sophisticated buyers did the deal." Verdict: lands, but v3 corrects the diagnosis. The two levers are NOT worth the same: the decomposition above shows BD ≈ 0pt and the exit multiple ≈ +2.9pt — they are not substitutes ("entry-flat exit OR funded BD" was wrong; the "OR" is deleted). The IC summary now presents: Conservative no-BD floor (~15%), Sponsor-consistent (entry-flat 12.9x exit: ~18%), Full-thesis (12.9x + 6.0x lev + bull ops: ~24%). The recommendation stands; the framing is corrected — the sponsor case is a bet on exit-multiple maintenance, not on the bolt-on machine.

B2. "Your ΔNWC at 15% of revenue growth is a guess sitting on the fastest-moving part of the model (US RD inventory absorbed €135m in 2025 alone)." Verdict: lands as a sensitivity gap. At 25% of Δrev, cumulative FCF drops ~€200m and exit leverage rises ~0.15x — IRR impact ~−0.6pt. Material but not thesis-breaking; flagged as a build-time refinement (input is editable at 03!B35).

B3. "Single-flow IRR flatters nothing but hides refinancing/dividend-recap optionality — and conversely ignores the 2026 stub period." We close 31-Dec-26 using FY26 guidance as the entry year; if closing slips into 2027 or FY26 misses guidance, entry economics shift. Verdict: partially lands; documented as a structural simplification. Recap optionality would only improve IRR (~+1pt) — conservative direction.

B4. "Isturisa is ~35–40% of RD by our own estimate, and relacorilant is a live Phase 3 threat. Where's the product-level bear?" Verdict: lands directionally. The bear scenario (RD +8%→5%) is the proxy; a true product-level model needs disclosure Recordati doesn't provide. Recorded as a known limitation with the register's L-label discipline.

Challenges that don't land

  • "12.9x is simply too expensive — Ipsen is 8.4x." The market cleared this exact multiple twice (2018, 2026), with the most sophisticated possible seller-buyer pair. Ipsen is the challenge case, not the proof; the sensitivity grid prices it (at 9–10x exit, IRR 2–7% — that IS the risk statement).
  • "100% sweep is unrealistic." It's the conservative modeling default; covenant-lite reality would allow retention, which only reallocates value, and the sweep input is editable.
  • "You can't validate against the actual debt structure." Correct until the CONSOB offer document publishes; the model's €5.45bn at bottom of the reported €5.5–6bn range is the right independent posture.

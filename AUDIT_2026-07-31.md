# Deep Audit — 31 July 2026

Second full pass over all eight pages. Scope: UI and responsive behaviour, accessibility, link and
asset integrity, cross-page number consistency, and a check for AI-generated writing.

Everything below was measured, not eyeballed. Layout and navigation claims were verified in headless
Chromium at 360 / 390 / 768 / 1280px. Contrast figures are computed WCAG 2.1 ratios. Arithmetic was
recomputed from the numbers as published.

**Headline:** the writing on the memo, both model pages and the three red-team pages holds up. The
sector map does not, on three separate counts (voice, sourcing, internal consistency). The UI had
one serious defect (the flagship memo was unusable on a phone) and a set of smaller ones, all now
fixed.

## 2. Does anything look AI-generated?

Checked all eight pages against the patterns `MEMO_PLAYBOOK.md` §4 already flags, plus the usual
markers: em dash density, negative parallelism, rule-of-three, superlative stacking, epigram cadence,
and AI-register vocabulary.

**Six pages pass.** The Dust memo, both model pages and the three red-team pages read as written and
then edited by a person. Em dash use is low (0 to 8 per file), the vocabulary scan came back
effectively clean, and the prose has the uneven texture of something revised in passes. The
red-team pages in particular read as genuinely argued rather than generated.

**The sector map does not pass.** It is the outlier on every measure.

*Em dash density.* 108 in one file. For comparison: technogym 7, recordati 8, home 6, red-team pages
0 to 1 each. Section 18 runs 5.4 per 100 words, section 3 runs 2.8. `MEMO_PLAYBOOK.md` §4 states the
rule directly: "No em dashes used as a crutch, they read as AI-generated."

*Rhetorical tics, all in the same file:*

- Negative parallelism: "This is not one industry. It is a chain of sequential monopolies."
- The "X IS the Y" reveal, twice: "the chip now IS the package", "The multiple spread IS the debate".
- Superlative triad: "the most concentrated, most geopolitically exposed, and most profitable
  industrial supply chain ever built". It appears three times (masthead, meta description, homepage
  card), so it is doing branding work as well.
- The knowing aside: "Ajinomoto (yes, the MSG company)".
- Verdict fragments reused as punchlines: "Revenue without economics" appears twice as a standalone
  judgment; "the moat and the burden are the same thing"; "Economics irrelevant".
- Framing labels: "The core insight:", "Rule of thumb:", "Pattern worth internalizing:", "the honest
  way to model it", "the disappearance test", "the truth serum", "the arms dealer that wins either
  way", "in its purest, riskiest form", "the quietest great business in semis", "Genuinely
  unknowable", "The meta-lesson of 80 years".

*The strongest single tell is cadence.* Every one of the 20 sections closes on an aphorism, at an
even density, across 725 lines. People do not sustain that when writing a long document over several
sittings; the epigrams cluster where the writer was engaged and thin out elsewhere. Here they never
thin out.

Two things travel with it and reinforce the read: it is the only research page on the site with no
citations at all, and the only one whose own numbers contradict each other (see §3).

**Call:** this is the site's own written standard, not an outside preference being imposed. Two
honest options. Rewrite it to the playbook's voice, cutting the em dashes and the section-closing
epigrams and letting the tables carry the argument. Or leave it unlisted until it is rewritten. What
should not happen is a page that reads as machine-written sitting one click from a homepage that
promises independent, cited work.

## 3. Number integrity

### Contradictions to resolve (not patched, because they need the source)

**a. $725B vs the components that are supposed to make it up.** This is the load-bearing figure of
the whole sector map. It appears 10 times and §10 calls it "the single most important number in
semis". Stage 8 breaks it out as AWS $200B, Google $175–185B, Meta $115–135B, Microsoft $110–120B.
That sums to **$600–640B**, against a stat band that labels $725B as "Big-4 hyperscaler capex 2026".
Two numbers for the same quantity, $85–125B apart, on the same page, unreconciled.
`MEMO_PLAYBOOK.md` §2: "Never let two numbers on the same page contradict each other without
addressing it." Either the $725B includes a second tier the label doesn't mention, or one set is
wrong. I did not guess which.

**b. Nvidia run-rate.** §8 states "~60% operating margin on ~$240B run-rate". §4 gives FY26 revenue
of $215.9B and Q1 FY27 of $81.6B, which annualises to $326B. §10 gives Q1 FY27 data-center revenue
of $75.2B, which annualises to $301B. $240B matches none of the three.

**c. Carl Zeiss Meditec is not a Zeiss optics proxy.** §14 lists it under hidden monopolies as a
route into the EUV optics business, calling the proxy "imperfect". Meditec is the listed ophthalmic
and microsurgery business. The lithography optics sit in Zeiss SMT, inside the unlisted Carl Zeiss
AG. It is not an imperfect proxy, it is a different business, and a reader who knows the sector will
stop reading there.

**d. Market-cap bands wide enough to read as unresearched.** SK Hynix at "~$600–900B" is a 50%
spread. Tokyo Electron "~$130–180B". Micron "~$1.0T" alongside prose saying it just crossed $1T. The
site's own confidence-tier discipline (High / Medium / Low / Not Disclosed) would handle this
cleanly; a band that wide with no date and no source does not.

## 4. Structural gaps that cost credibility

**1. The citation claim is not true of most of the site.** `README.md` says "Every number is cited to
a public source" and "Every claim traces to a citation. No exceptions." In practice the Dust memo
carries 19 source links and **technogym, recordati, all three red-team pages and the sector map carry
zero**. Five of six research pages fall short of the standard the homepage advertises. A reader who
checks finds the claim overstated, which is worse than never making it. This is the highest-value
remaining fix on the site.

**2. The red-team differentiator doesn't cover the flagship.** `README.md`: "Every piece is expected
to carry ... a red-team page attacking its own assumptions." The red-team index: "Every future model
gets its own review here." Dust has no red-team page and does not link to one, and neither does the
sector map. The site's stated differentiator covers two of four pieces, and it misses the one the
homepage tells visitors to read first.

**3. The sector map shares nothing with the house design system but the favicon.** Its only
occurrences of `#0F172A` and `#D4AF37` are inside the favicon data URI. Light theme, Georgia serif,
`#1a5cff` blue accent, its own navigation, no sticky house topnav, no "Last updated" line, no
30-Second Read in the house pattern. `MEMO_PLAYBOOK.md` §6: "Match the existing five pages' visual
system exactly ... don't introduce a new style per piece." Clicking from the homepage into it feels
like leaving the site.

**4. "WORKING DRAFT / updated version coming" is a liability, by the site's own reasoning.**
`SITE_AUDIT.md` rejected progress trackers because they become "stale-looking the moment it isn't
updated; a liability, not a feature." A draft badge on a portfolio piece is the same thing: it tells
a recruiter this is unfinished and invites them to skip it. Finish it or unlist it.

**5. No `404.html`.** GitHub Pages falls back to its own error page, which breaks the visual identity
and dead-ends the visitor. Twelve lines in the house style, linking home, closes it.

## 5. What research to add next

The portfolio is four pieces in four formats. Breadth is not the gap. The gap a professional reader
notices is **follow-through**: nothing on the site says whether any of the calls turned out right.

**1. Write the Technogym scorecard now.** `IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md` item 3 defers this to January
2027. That is out of date: H1-2026 results printed **30 July 2026, yesterday**. The Technogym page
conditions its entire rating on exactly this event ("A BUY rating requires the upcoming H1-2026
earnings report (due 30 July) to confirm that the Americas growth slowdown has stabilized"). Until
it is written up, the site's most prominent rating is visibly stale against a catalyst it named
itself. Write it even if the call was wrong, especially if the call was wrong. Nobody else does this,
and it is the single strongest proof that the process is real rather than performed. The Recordati
CONSOB offer document is the same trigger, on its own timing.

**2. Retrofit citations on the five uncited pages.** Not new research, but it is what makes the
existing research count, and it removes a claim on the homepage that currently doesn't hold.

**3. Red-team the Dust memo.** The attacks are already visible inside the memo and would be quick to
write honestly: ARR is company-reported and undated within "early 2026"; the $350–500M post is an
internal estimate that drives the entire sizing recommendation; the MuleSoft / Segment / Looker
comparison set is three hand-picked outcomes presented as a base rate.

**4. Only then, a fifth piece.** There is a real fork here and it depends on the recruiting target:

- *Venture / growth track:* a second VC memo. Both existing valuations are Milan-listed mid-caps,
  which reads narrow, and the memo format is what actually gets tested. A European enterprise-software
  or fintech Series A/B, run through the same five-stage skeleton, would balance the portfolio.
- *IB / PE track:* a merger model with accretion/dilution. The kit has a DCF and an LBO but no M&A
  model, `MODEL_PLAYBOOK.md` is built to be adapted, and it is the third model every banking
  interview expects. This is the higher-value addition if the target is banking rather than venture.

Default recommendation if the target is unstated: the merger model, because it closes a visible hole
in the modelling kit, whereas a second memo repeats a format the site already demonstrates.

**5. Chassis: keep saying no.** Search, tag filters, theme toggle, RSS, schema.org, keyboard
shortcuts and progress bars were rejected in `SITE_AUDIT.md` and are still the right call at four
pieces. Not relitigating. The only two chassis items worth adding now are the `404.html` and the
citation retrofit above.

## 6. Method

- Rendering, overflow and anchor behaviour: headless Chromium, four viewport widths, per-element
  bounding-box checks against the viewport edge, with elements inside deliberate scroll containers
  excluded.
- Contrast: computed WCAG 2.1 relative-luminance ratios for every text/background pair in use.
- Links and assets: every `href` and `src` on all eight pages resolved against the filesystem; every
  in-page anchor resolved against the document's own `id`s.
- Arithmetic: SVG bar geometry decoded against each chart's declared scale; all published tables and
  bridges recomputed.
- Writing: em dash density per file and per section, plus pattern scans for negative parallelism,
  superlative triads, "X IS the Y" constructions and AI-register vocabulary, benchmarked across pages
  so the sector map's scores are relative to the author's own baseline rather than an external one.

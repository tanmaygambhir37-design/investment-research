Recommendation

PARTICIPATE selectively as a co-investor (6/10). Base: ~15.0% IRR / ~2.01x MOIC (12.6x entry on close-date net debt; 12.9x on FY25A gross, announced basis; 5.5x leverage, 11.5x exit, BD €400m/yr 2027–2030). No-BD conservative floor ~15.1%. Sponsor-consistent read (entry-flat 12.9x exit): ~18%. Full thesis (12.9x + 6.0x leverage + bull ops): ~24%. Decline as a standalone 20%+-hurdle underwrite. For minorities: tender — €51.29 certain cash beats underwrite risk without control rights. Bear ~4% / Bull ~24%.

Business & Deal

Two businesses on one P&L: Specialty & Primary Care (~€1.54bn, +2–3%, the bond — brand/distribution cash cow) funding Rare Diseases (~€1.08bn, +29.7% in 2025, the equity — orphan pricing power, US-led, Isturisa peak-sales doubled to >€1.2bn in Nov-25). Deal: €51.29/share ex-div (€52.00 cum), €10.73bn equity, EV ~€12.49bn = 12.6x FY25 EBITDA on close-date net debt (12.9x on FY25A gross), 12.89% premium (low — Rossini's 46.82% pre-committed). Effectively a CVC VII → CVC IX continuation with GBL co-control and ADIA/CPPIB/PSP/StepStone co-invest. Critical precedent: CVC VII entered 2018 at €28.00 = the same 12.9x — eight years, zero multiple expansion, all return from EBITDA doubling plus dividends.

BD Value-Creation Decomposition (the v3 correction)

No-BD floor ~15.1% IRR. Funding BD €400m/yr adds ~0 to −0.1pt — the 9x-in / 11.5x-out multiple arbitrage is almost exactly offset by interest drag on un-swept cash plus forfeited deleveraging (BD grows MOIC dollars, not the rate). Exit at entry-flat 12.9x adds +2.9pt. The exit multiple is ~100% of the sponsor-consistent case; the earlier "entry-flat exit OR funded BD" framing was wrong — they are not substitutes, and the "OR" is deleted. (2031 exit-year BD spend removed: it would have bought EBITDA only in 2032, post-exit; cumulative BD is €1.6bn at a true 9.0x effective.)

Risks

(1) Exit-multiple compression toward Ipsen's 8.4x — dominant: at 10x exit IRR is low single digits (~4%); (2) Isturisa (~35–40% of RD, est.) vs Corcept's relacorilant; (3) FY26 margin guide-down (37.8%→36.5%) proving structural rather than investment/FX; (4) US pricing policy and tariffs; (5) SPC decay acceleration; (6) adverse selection — the best-informed seller chose to sell (mitigated by chairman rollover and new institutional checks; priced via our exit haircut).

Independent Model vs Actual Deal — What Explains the Gap

Our €5.45bn debt sits at the bottom of the reported €5.5–6.0bn package; entry multiple identical (deal fact). The gap to the sponsors' ~18% is almost entirely the exit multiple we deliberately haircut: entry-flat 12.9x exit = +2.9pt (the dominant lever). Funded BD redeployment is close to IRR-neutral (their base, our bull — it lifts MOIC, not the rate); 6.0x opening leverage adds ~1pt; Isturisa underwritten to its raised >€1.2bn peak adds 2–3pts of EBITDA CAGR. Conclusion: at our conservative floor the deal clears mid-teens; their return case requires holding the multiple at entry — which is precisely why the asset went to the sponsor who has run it since 2018.

What Changes My Mind

Positive: relacorilant failure; offer document showing cheaper debt; first post-close bolt-on below 9x. Negative: FY26 margin below 36%; Isturisa US deceleration; exit-window deterioration 2030–31. Red-team / version revisions applied: three-case framing (our base labelled a conservative floor, not a midpoint); BD value-creation decomposed (BD ≈ 0pt vs exit-multiple +2.9pt); 2031 exit-year BD bug fixed; entry net debt bridged to close-date; single-flow IRR noted as conservative vs recap optionality (~+1pt); 7-year continuation-hold deferred pending recap + cash-interest-on-excess modeling.
